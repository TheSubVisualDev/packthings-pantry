# Site audit, 14 Sep 2026

Two independent auditors walked the app, one on the kitchen half and one on the
recipe half. Neither was told what had already been found, which code was
recent, or who wrote it.

That matters. A first pass done by the same agent that had spent the day
changing the app found nothing wrong with anything it had just written, which
is not a plausible result. Every finding in that pass landed in code it had not
touched. The split below exists to break that.

**Status:** all three auditors reported, and everything below is done and
pushed. A third auditor was added afterwards for UI and UX specifically,
because the first two returned mostly architectural suggestions dressed as
design ones; its critique is in its own section at the end.

Where a finding turned out to be something other than what was reported, that
is recorded rather than quietly closed.

Severity is blocker / friction / polish, worst first.

## Auditor 1 — the kitchen half

### 1. Expiry maths disagrees with itself, three ways · blocker · DONE `9844e01`

One item reads "2 days ago" on its own page, "2d ago" on `/pantry/expiring`,
and "3d over" on `/stats`. Tiger Bloomer's use-by is 2026-09-13 and today is
the 14th, so the true answer is one day.

Two unrelated computations exist and disagree by construction:

- `lib/dates.ts:23-27` — `daysUntil()` parses `` `${date}T00:00:00` `` with no
  `Z`, so local midnight, then `Math.floor`. Floor rounds AWAY from zero on a
  negative, so yesterday afternoon comes out as -2. Feeds `item-detail.tsx:125`
  and `lib/stats.ts:163`.
- `lib/queries.ts:517` — `CAST(julianday(use_by) - julianday('now') AS INTEGER)`
  truncates TOWARD zero, so the same moment comes out as -1. Feeds
  `/pantry/expiring` and `use-it-up.tsx`.

`parseStamp`, four lines above `daysUntil` in the same file, explicitly appends
`Z` for UTC. So the file contradicts its own convention on the line below it.

Verified independently before recording: both readings reproduce from the
source, and `Math.floor` is the wrong one of the two — it over-reports by a day
for any part of the day that is not exactly midnight, which is all of it.

This is the same failure class AGENTS.md already tracks for the stock cascade,
"the rule written twice". Nobody wrote it down for dates. Whatever the fix, it
ends with one implementation and a note in AGENTS.md.

### 2. The shop filter does not filter Running Low · blocker · DONE `69e7413`

`/pantry/list`, tap "Tesco". The subtitle correctly becomes "What Tesco has,
plus anything you can get anywhere" — and the Running Low panel still lists
both soy sauces, which are tagged Asian supermarket, with "Add all 5" still
saying 5.

`app/pantry/list/page.tsx:47` calls `getRestockSuggestions(kitchen.id)` with no
filter, while line 46's `getList` does get one. `RestockPanel` (line 86) is
never passed `filter` at all.

A control that visibly engages and changes nothing is worse than one that is
absent.

### 3. Grouping by Nutrition or Place leaves dead gaps · friction · DONE `2cdc287`

`/pantry?by=nutrition` and `?by=location`: rows fade out partway down a group,
then 400-600px of blank space inside the same white card before the next
heading. Tag grouping, the default, never does it. Reproduced on two
independent fresh loads, so not a compile-timing fluke.

Suspected cause is the `.stagger` rise-in (`app/globals.css:307-319`, applied
per group at `components/stock-list.tsx:199`) leaving trailing children at
their opacity-0 frame — invisible, still taking layout height.

**This is in code written the same day as the audit, and the pass that missed
it was done by the agent that wrote it.** Recorded here rather than quietly
fixed, because the miss is the more useful half of the finding.

### 4. "0 to buy" contradicts the panel beneath it · friction · DONE `69e7413`

`/pantry/list` header says "0 to buy" directly above a Running Low card
offering "Add all 5 to the list". Technically consistent — the header counts
only what is on the list — but it reads as the page disagreeing with itself.
`app/pantry/list/page.tsx:53,57`.

Corroborated: found independently by the first pass too.

### 5. `/tonight` filter chips clip mid-word · polish · DONE `1dfffd6`

"Baking · Frying · under 15 min · under 30 min · und…" runs off the viewport
with no fade or arrow to say the row scrolls.

### Checked and sound

Add-item end to end (four taps to a saved item, the "Just add it" shortcut
works); item delete from the ⋯ menu; empty states on `/pantry`, `/pantry/list`
and `/tonight` for a fresh kitchen; an item at "none left" staying visible
because it has a restock target, which is deliberate and documented at
`app/pantry/page.tsx:148-170`; People and Discover structurally sound.

### Radical suggestions

**Merge `/tonight` and `/plan`.** Both answer "what should I cook", on two
separate suggestion engines — `rankTonight` and the planner's "Fill the gaps" —
which can already disagree with each other the same way the date code does. One
screen, defaulting to tonight, moving by day. Removes a nav slot and a whole
class of future sync bugs. Cost: real rearchitecture. Risk: `/tonight` is tuned
for one fast decision at six o'clock when you are hungry, and a view that also
browses a week could easily lose that.

**Read the use-by date with the camera instead of the keyboard.** The expiry
field is a date picker for a number printed in large type on the packet the
person is holding, and the app already runs Tesseract client-side for receipts.
Cost is low because the OCR investment exists. Risk: a barcode or allergen
number misread as a date is worse than an empty field, so it must confirm
before saving and never silently autofill.

## Auditor 2 — the recipe half

Walked with Playwright, reading values out of the DOM rather than eyeballing
screenshots, and cooked recipe 6 through all nine steps to the end.

### 1. The recipe page promises stock the cook action cannot find · blocker · DONE `8e19259`, WITH A CORRECTION

Recipe 6 shows `3 Sweet Peppers — Low` and `1 Brown Onions — Low`. "Low" only
renders when a pantry item WAS matched and is merely short
(`components/cook-panel.tsx:163-172`). Cooking it then said "Sweet Peppers,
Brown Onions, Cannellini Beans Water could not be worked out from the recipe",
and deducted nothing for the first two.

Three different answers to "which pantry item does this ingredient mean":

- `app/recipes/[id]/page.tsx:126-131` trusts `recipe_ingredients.item_id`, else
  an exact-lowercase name match.
- `app/recipes/[id]/actions.ts:163-211` reads `cookbook_links`, and only on a
  miss falls through to the real resolver.
- `lib/pantry-match.ts` is the real resolver, and correctly refuses a
  low-confidence guess.

`lib/pantry-match.ts`'s own header says this class of bug — "four places decided
this independently and agreed only by being equally wrong" — was fixed by
centralising on one resolver. The recipe page never adopted it. So a stale
`item_id` lets the page assert a match the cook action will not honour, and you
find out in small print under the celebration screen.

### 2. Numbered steps that wrap get shredded mid-sentence · blocker · DONE `b0ec082`

An 8-step numbered method, hard-wrapped across two physical lines each — which
is what pasting off any blog or PDF gives you — came out as **11 steps**, cut
mid-word. Step 1 ended "...Season the", step 2 began "chicken and brown it...".

`lib/recipe-text.ts:899` — `asParagraphs = stepLines.length <= 2` — treats any
method with more than two physical lines as already one-per-instruction and
leaves it alone. It never rejoins a numbered item's continuation line.

**That line was written today.** The rejoining case was never considered.

### 3. A wrapped headnote is split across two unrelated fields, silently · blocker · DONE `b0ec082`

A four-line paragraph became: description = the first line, truncated
mid-sentence with no closing punctuation; Notes ("what happened last time you
made it") = the rest of the same sentence.

`lib/recipe-text.ts:800-812` only captures a description from a single line
under the title. `looksLikeStep()` calls anything twelve words or longer a step,
so the long first line falls to the branch at `:844-850`, which puts line one in
`description` and every later line of the same paragraph into `extraNotes`. No
warning anywhere.

### 4. Cook time is silently overwritten by prep time · blocker · DONE `b0ec082`

`Prep time: 15 minutes` / `Cook time: 35 mins` saves as prep 15, **cook 15**.

`readTotalMinutes()` (`lib/recipe-text.ts:594`) has bare `time` in its match
words, so it matches the PREP line, and `:775` gives it the cook slot. By the
time the real cook line is read, `cook` is non-null and `:753` discards it.
First-line-wins, with no way for a later and more specific line to correct it.

**This is a regression introduced today, in the same commit that added a check
suite for it.** The suite tested "Takes 40 minutes" and never tested a recipe
carrying both a prep line and a cook line. Reproduced in one command before
recording.

### 5. Size and measure phrasing pollutes ingredient names · friction · DONE `b0ec082`

"1-inch piece of ginger, grated" stores as **"Inch piece of ginger"**. Not
hypothetical: recipe 10 already holds ingredient 7 as **"Teaspoon of cinnamon"**,
unmeasured. It will never match Cinnamon on the shelf and never comes off stock.
`tidyName()` / `readIngredientLine()` (`lib/recipe-text.ts:238-273`) don't strip
"N-inch piece of" or "teaspoon of" before taking the trailing noun.

### 6. A blank ingredient row fails with an error you cannot act on · friction · DONE `84b7ce5`

Leaving a fourth row empty fails the save with
`ingredients[3].item_name: Missing item_name.` — raw, shown on the Details
screen, which does not display ingredients, with no way back to row 4.
`components/recipe-editor.tsx:201-242` sends every row verbatim instead of
pruning empty ones, which is what the paste reader already does with blank
lines.

### 7. A new recipe cannot be named until every ingredient and step is written · friction · DONE `84b7ce5`

The stages run Ingredients → Method → "What is it called?". The escape hatch,
"Skip to the details", renders only `{!last && recipeId && ...}`
(`components/recipe-editor.tsx:1004`) — so it exists when editing and never on
`/recipes/new`. You must page through every ingredient and every step before you
can type a title.

**Also written today**, and the gate was deliberate. The reasoning was that an
edit is often one field at the end. It did not account for wanting to name the
thing you are making before you make it.

### 8. "Maybe:" tag suggestions are not about the recipe · friction · DONE `84b7ce5`

`app/recipes/[id]/page.tsx:346-349` appends every tag the user has ever used to
the suggestions, unconditionally. A soup and a tofu stir-fry were both offered
"Baking" and "Frying" — simply the two most-used tags, echoed back.
`lib/recipe-tags.ts` says in its own comments that every suggestion should be
checkable against the row.

### 9-11. Polish · DONE `42e6265`, `1dfffd6`

- **"1 Brown Onions"** on the recipe page and the cook screen. Names are stored
  plural for the stock list and never singularised at quantity 1.
- **The preparation-note field clips silently** — "pressed and cubed" shows as
  "pressed and cube". Nothing lost, just invisible.
- **All-caps pasted titles are never normalised**, so recipe 10 shouts in the
  breadcrumb, the page title and the cook screen. Corroborates the first pass.

### Checked and sound

The `/recipes` list, its tabs, search, filters and empty states. The cook
step-by-step screen itself. Nutrition maths and its "N of these are estimates"
disclosure, correct in both test cases.

### Radical suggestions

**One resolver for "which pantry item is this".** Delete `item_id` as a trusted
read path, make every screen call the live resolver, keep exactly one table for
human overrides. Cost: a migration and four call sites. Risk: some "Low" badges
correctly become "to buy", which will look like the app forgetting things it
knew.

**Delete the choice between writing and pasting.** They are the same problem in
two UIs. Make `/recipes/new` be the paste box, parsing even a single hand-typed
line live. Only viable once the reader is hardened — findings 2 to 4 say it is
not — or you force every manual entry through code proven to mangle ordinary
input.

**Make cooking a mode on the recipe page, not a route.** `/recipes/[id]` and
`/recipes/[id]/cook` compute shelf state separately, which is the shape of
finding 1 at the UI layer.

## The correction on auditor 2, finding 1

Worth reading before trusting any of this again.

The report was that the recipe page prints "Low" beside Sweet Peppers and
Brown Onions, and then cooking says they "could not be worked out from the
recipe" and deducts nothing. The diagnosis - three different answers to which
pantry item an ingredient means - was correct as a fact about the code, and
the page has been put on the shared resolver.

But the symptom does not reproduce. Both lines resolve, and both convert
cleanly: 3 count against a count row, 1 count against a count row. Cooking the
soup in a browser afterwards reported "Sweet Peppers - needed 3count, only
0count in stock", which is a shortage, correctly matched and correctly
measured. The recipe reads "cooked 3x" and did not before the audit ran.

The auditor cooked the recipe repeatedly while testing it, emptied the shelf,
and then reported the empty shelf as a matching failure. The split brain was
real and worth fixing on its own merits. The bug it was blamed for was the
audit's own footprint.

**The lesson is general: an agent that walks flows for real changes the data it
is judging.** Both walking auditors left rows behind - seven test recipes, an
item called "Test Olive Oil Audit", three extra cooks of recipe 6 - and all of
it had to be found and removed by hand afterwards. Next time, either give them
a cloned database or tell them to record what they create as they go.

## The UX auditor

Added after the first two, because both of them answered a design brief with
architecture. Its verdict: "the bones are premium - Manrope, real
view-transition choreography, a genuinely lovely dark full-bleed cook mode -
but almost every non-cook screen is a list of cards in card order rather than
a designed hierarchy."

Everything it raised is done: `/tonight` is the front door, the Tonight banner
is off the shelf, Discover stops listing your own recipes back at you, the
ingredient row is three fields instead of six, cooking has a payoff, and the
stock rows take a swipe in both directions.

Its two remaining ideas are projects rather than fixes, and are listed in
PROGRESS.md.
