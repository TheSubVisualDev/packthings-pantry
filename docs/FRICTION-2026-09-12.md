# The friction pass

Walked every route, every form and every mode of interaction on 12 Sep 2026, at
Luna's request, before the design pass. The question asked of each screen was
not "is this usable" but "what does this make me tell it that it could have
worked out".

The principle, which is worth stating once rather than repeating:

> **The app should know what your kitchen is like.** Every field it asks for
> twice, every default that ignores what you already own, and every screen that
> hands you a list instead of an answer is the app making you do its filing.

Three rules fell out of doing it, and they are the ones to hold to in the
design pass:

1. **A guess may only land in a field nobody has touched.** Typing something
   and watching it change back teaches people to distrust every clever form
   they meet afterwards.
2. **Say where the guess came from.** "Filled in like your Soy sauce" with one
   button to take it back. A form that fills itself in silently is a form you
   stop reading, and then a wrong guess ships.
3. **Move before the server does.** The database is in Nuremberg. Anything that
   waits for a round trip before showing a result feels slower than doing it by
   hand, whatever the actual numbers say.

## Done

### The add form fills itself in — `f5974ac`

Six decisions deep on a screen that should be one. Type a name and the nearest
thing you already own hands over its unit, shelf, tags, shop, pack size and how
long it keeps once open; nothing similar falls back to the generic-food table,
which at least knows whether a thing is counted or weighed. `lib/suggest.ts`,
checked by `npm run check:suggest`.

It also catches a duplicate before it exists. A pantry holding both Tomatoes
and Tomatos has every recipe match, rescue and shopping list quietly half
right, and somebody typing the second one is the only moment it is cheap to
stop. Duplicates are **identical stemmed token sets**, not a similarity score -
a score called "Dark soy sauce" a duplicate of "Soy sauce", which is a second
bottle rather than a mistake.

### The shopping list survives a burst — `4111333`

Cleared and refocused on submit rather than after the round trip; names offered
from the pantry so a line matches the row it means; unit follows the name
instead of always being grams.

### Opening a pack asks the question that lights the rescue panel

`shelf_life_days` was null for nearly every item, and the deadline on an open
jar is `opened_at` plus that - so an open jar had no deadline and "use these
up" was structurally dark for most of a pantry. Nothing ever asked for it.
Opening a pack now does, once per item, at the one moment somebody is holding
the jar. Never guessed: an invented number about how long food keeps is worse
than no number.

### The restock target stopped being a sum

It is the field that makes the shopping list offer things unasked, and almost
nothing had one - because it is a number in a unit you have to stop and reason
about ("how many grams of butter is a sensible amount to keep in?"). It is now
one tap: **1 pack / 2 packs / 3 packs** for anything packaged, or **what's in
now** for anything loose, which is usually about right and needs no history to
work out.

### Adjust searches by rank, not just by substring

Typing "tom" used to hide everything without those letters and leave the
survivors alphabetical, so Tinned tomatoes sat above Tomatoes. It uses the same
scorer as the rest of the app now. Substring matches that score nothing stay,
ranked below, because you may well have meant them.

### Scan and guess stopped fighting

The barcode prefill used to switch the guessing off entirely. It resolves per
field now - what you typed, then what the scan read off the packet, then what
the kitchen reckons - so a scan still fills in the pack size it knows and the
kitchen still fills in the shelf and the tags it does not.

## Still to do, worst first

### 1. Selection mode and the bulk bar were never designed

Noted in the phase 3 handover and still true. Entering selection mode, what is
selectable, and what the bar can do are three separate discoveries.

### 2. Receipt review is one screen doing two jobs

It reads a photo and then asks about every line. The matching is good enough
since M1 to accept the confident ones silently and only ask about the rest -
the same shape as the cookbook's linking prompt, which is the pattern this app
should reuse rather than invent a second time.

### 3. The three tab patterns

The header's segmented control, the editor's preview/fields toggle and the
stock page's group-by switch are three answers to one question. This is a
design-pass item rather than a friction one, but it belongs on the same list:
every one of them is a thing to learn separately.

### 4. Smaller, ranked, not yet done

- The recipe editor has 18 inputs and no guessing at all. Units per ingredient
  line could follow the name the way the add form now does.
- Nothing in the app is reachable by keyboard shortcut, and the two things
  done most - adjust stock, add to list - are three taps deep from anywhere.
- The FAB opens a menu of four things; after a month of use the app knows which
  one this person actually uses.
- No empty state anywhere offers the thing that would fix it except `/tonight`.
